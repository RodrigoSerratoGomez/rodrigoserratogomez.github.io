#1

the core of the factoring product

It lived in the company's heaviest monolith, and the debt had been there for a long time. Product leadership made it a priority.

In sprint planning I volunteered. I was a junior, but I had the most time on the product in a team of two juniors and one mid.
The diagnosis

I was looking in the wrong place.

✕ What I assumed
  • logic inside controllers
  • repeated methods
  • hard-coded values
  • no clear responsibilities

"It's the backend code."

✓ What debugging showed
  • the code was a mess, yes
  • but the weight was in the DB
  • stored procedures doing the heavy lifting
  • over the product's largest tables

"It's the data path."

My first pass went straight into the code — no logs, no look at the tables, no check of the frontend. A full debugging session corrected the diagnosis. Then I wrote a step-by-step restructuring plan and validated it with the interim tech lead before changing a line.

The work

Four steps, in this order.

01

Clear the path to the database

Removed inefficient call loops, added validations to stop endless loads, and — with the acting product owner — cut deprecated business logic. About 40% of the backend.

02

Fix the data layer

Rewrote the heaviest stored procedures, and wrote new ones where other modules still depended on the old. Added missing indexes; dropped unused ones only after checking every dependency.

≈ −65% latency on procedure calls
03

Finish the backend

The remaining 60% of the restructure, each endpoint tested through Swagger.

04

Redesign the frontend

With the UX/UI lead: pagination, new filters, clearer feedback. Business validations moved to the backend. Signed off by the CPO.

How it was measured
  • DB: SQL Server statistics and Query Store, before and after — run with the interim tech lead.
  • UX: end-user stopwatch, Staging with the fix vs Production without it, side by side.
  • Prod: final check in production, 242 s → 12 s.
What I learned

Estimated: 2 weeks. Real: ~5.

The two weeks were a guess, made before any analysis: about four weeks of build plus four to five days waiting for product sign-off.

What I'd do differently

I left the frontend for last. Moving its business validations to the backend meant reopening a backend I had just finished — more code, more tests. Today I map the whole path, UI included, before changing the first line.

My part · 90%

Analysis, plan, build and fix. The interim tech lead validated the plan and ran the DB measurements with me.

The outcome

The fix reached the CEO. Together with my habit of asking for the hardest tickets, it led to my promotion to Mid-Level soon after.
